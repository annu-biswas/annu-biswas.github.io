Every first-year seminar begins the same way. I hand out a shipping manifest and ask what it tells us. The answers are always variations on *nothing*.

## The turn

By week six the same students can read a manifest and tell me the size of a crew, the length of a voyage, the price of provisions, and - from the gaps - who was not being counted. Nothing about the document changed. What changed is that they stopped waiting for it to be interesting and started asking it questions.

## Why it matters beyond the classroom

Most of the past is boring documents. If we only teach the diaries and the letters, we train historians to work on the two percent of the record that was written to be read.
